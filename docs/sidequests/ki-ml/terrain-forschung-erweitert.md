# Terrain Research: Quality, Control, and Landscape as History

As of 2026-11-12. **Research and own research proposals, no model sign-off.** Primary sources, model cards, configs, and small weight headers were inspected; no model trained or executed, no large weights loaded. The concepts below are not yet implemented.

## Course Correction and Selection

**We should explore ML as an additional design capability: generating coherent landscapes, enabling targeted interventions, and learning traces of evolution.** Ten seconds on Apple M1 was, per explicit course correction from the author, a metaphor rather than a gate. GPU machines, longer offline simulations, and models under one billion parameters are permissible. Quality gain can justify training even without a speed gain.

The [previous M1 pilot plan](terrain-architektur-plan.md) remains unchanged as a historical, narrowly scoped option for accelerating a form filter. Its rule "direct TS filter fast enough → ML dropped" does **not** apply to the new research choice. Just as little does a poor current generator prove that a suitable model family would be unsuitable. [Research 107](../../research/107-landschaft-stadt-evolution.md) documents rejected landscapes: they provide negative examples and comparison cases, not a sole quality teacher.

Priority goes to four combinable yet individually falsifiable directions:

1. **Graph and sketch jointly condition terrain, water, and terrace fields.** Best combination of author control and new forms; first own training trial.
2. **Regional terrain priors with persistent local edits.** Test Terrain Diffusion in full instead of looking only at its small coarse building block; existing weights lower the barrier to entry.
3. **Inverse landscape design with author ranking.** Repeatedly modify the same landscape in a targeted way and search variants by intent, existing-stock protection, and preferences.
4. **Learned evolution operator.** Largest research potential for history and interventions, but hardest data and long-term validation; start separately.

## Four Own Concepts

All sizes and compute times in these profiles are **search ranges and bounded trial budgets**, not measured requirements. Profile 200–1,000 steps first; derive throughput, peak VRAM, and remaining runtime from that. An exhausted budget ends that trial, not the entire model family.

### A · River Skeleton and Sketch Jointly Become Landscape

**What does it learn?** A conditional distribution of plausible valley cross-sections, terraces, confluences, and slope forms for a given spatial intent. It should deliver multiple credible solutions for the same sketch. Compared to noise, the hoped-for new capability is the joint distribution of these structures; compared to our generator, more form variation without extra hand-written case rules. Blind comparison must demonstrate this.

**Input → Output.** Directed river graph with outlets, external catchment, width/elevation anchors; author strokes for floodplain, terrace, ridges; regional elevation, initial terrain, protected existing stock, and edit mask. Rasterize graph features initially as distance, direction, elevation, and catchment channels. Output at 256²: elevation in meters, terrace/floodplain probability, river corridor, and optionally log drainage area as auxiliary task. A water-probability channel is neither water level nor river bed. Graph IDs remain input data; new topology is an explicitly separate proposal.

**Architecture/Training.** Custom 30–150M terrain U-Net with 2–20M condition encoder; alternatively Talus with roughly 23M base parameters or later the 253.7M Terrain Diffusion base plus adapter. Initialize new channels and heads, freeze backbone first, then selectively fine-tune. Talus's 64²/64 m contract cannot be turned into a 16 m contract by resizing: scale change requires new data and evaluation. As an own research variant, **graph-conditioned flow matching** replaces the denoising loss: graph encoder supplies catchment tokens, the field network learns a conditioned transport velocity between noise and terrain. This generation time is not geomorphological time; fewer solver evaluations are a hypothesis to be tested.

**Data and Loss.** Real metric DEM crops supply form priors; derived river graphs are algorithmic pseudolabels. Slightly imprecise sketches of the same terrain train input tolerance. A genuine graph shift, by contrast, needs a new matching target: locally deform/incise initial DEM, numerically relax with locked exterior, check the result, and derive target graph and auxiliary fields from it. Stored is the triple initial terrain, change intent, new target terrain. Pairing the shifted graph with the unchanged DEM would be false supervision. Contradictory elevation/protection specs get a conflict label and no positive reconstruction target; conflict detection remains external initially. Derive floodplain/terrace labels first from explicit profile rules plus manual spot checks and mark as pseudolabels; mask out uncertain areas in the auxiliary loss. Denoising/flow loss plus metric elevation/gradient errors, mask preservation, boundary consistency, and auxiliary-task loss; slope and balance penalties supplement, but do not replace a final check. Do not train solely on perfect sketches extracted from the target: that would obscure the later input contract.

**Falsification/PoC.** Unseen catchments, freehand reverse bends, and a confluence with conflicting elevations. Compare full conditioning, removed conditions, and swapped graphs at identical randomness. If only the auxiliary image responds and not the elevation, or the model satisfies conditions only by collapsing to one shape, the hypothesis has failed. Minimal: 128² and only elevation + river corridor, 1,000–5,000 distinct landscape crops, spatially separated tests; four manually defined intervention types.

**Effort/Rights.** Weeks for adapter pilot, months for solid joint hydrology. Starting assumption 24–48 GB GPU, 20–100 GPU hours as pilot cap, data preparation additional. Talus Apache-2.0 and Terrain Diffusion MIT respectively; own weights inherit the obligations of their actual base. License and document real data, pseudolabels, and simulation data separately.

### B · Landscape Ages Under a Learned Operator

**What does it learn?** A temporal state transition under known drivers: what elevation, sediment volume, and water distribution follow after a given interval. New would be the coupled, state-dependent response to precipitation, material, and interventions, rather than a one-off pretty surface. Slow offline simulation may initially generate better landscapes directly; the network is additionally interesting for search, variation, and optional later distillation.

**Input → Output.** Elevation, water level/depth, mobile sediment volume, material/erodibility field, precipitation, boundary inflow, timestep, and intervention mask → changes in these fields plus boundary fluxes. Volume/mass, time, and units explicit. Embankment or cutoff are recorded as external interventions. Geological millions of years and urban flood events are different data regimes; a stream-power teacher proves neither flood nor meander dynamics.

**Architecture/Training.** From-scratch FNO/U-Net hybrid, 5–80M parameters: global modes for catchment coupling, local convolution for steep banks. Alternative NCA with 0.5–10M parameters learns local flow transfers over many steps. Small NCAs do not automatically mean low cost due to rollout length. For open boundaries, do not simply close FNO periodically; test boundary masks, padding, and explicit fluxes. A conservative flux head with discrete divergence can structure balances, but cannot enforce correct physics alone.

**Data/Loss.** Real DEMs as initial states; simulations of varying parameters and at least two suitable process families provide sequences. Landlab/Fastscape are possible teachers for selected processes, not a universal hydrology model. First single-step, then multi-step training with elevation/sediment error, boundary balance, non-negativity, and errors on key cross-sections. Normalize changes separately from absolute elevations so mere persistence does not dominate the loss. On true rock ledges, do not apply a blanket smoothing penalty; breaklines/material masks need dedicated handling. In addition, develop an own 128² simulation on GPU if the chosen process admits a parallel discrete form; test that teacher against known reference cases. Real DEM time pairs would be external evidence, but without boundary conditions provide no unambiguous causes.

**Falsification/PoC.** Against persistence, simple smoothing, and direct simulator; identical initial surfaces with halved rain, different material, and shifted outlet. After 10/100/1,000 steps check mass error, sinks, outlet switches, and form statistics, not just mean pixel loss. Minimal: 100–300 short 128² sequences, one clearly named erosion equation, held-out parameter ranges. The pilot may claim only that process. A pretty single state with an exploding rollout refutes the evolution claim.

**Effort/Rights.** Weeks for narrow operator, months for river migration plus human regulation. Assumption 24–48 GB GPU, 10–80 GPU hours pilot training; measure and budget simulator time separately. Own initial weights; verify rights of teacher programs and data separately. Distillation only after convincing long-term behavior, if warranted into a 5–30M single-step student. It is a later delivery path, not a research goal.

### C · Shaping Landscape Backward: Intent Instead of Slider Lottery

**What does it learn?** Initially only a ranking of which variant best matches the author given the same intent. Later a conditional change distribution. Example: "This terrace needs more contiguous buildable area; river, old settlement, and the opposite slope stay." This opens targeted trade-off search instead of full regeneration. Author preference is a design judgment, not a hydrological label.

**Input → Output.** Persistent initial terrain, protection mask, river graph, target intervals for terrace area/relief, and local change intent → multiple field deltas along with measured target deviation and changed area. Apply hard existing-stock locks externally; show unsolvable demands as a conflict. No silent replacement of building or water identities.

**Architecture/Training.** Immediately without new generative training: search over latents or conditions of an existing terrain model, exact measurements plus 1–10M ranker. Ranking from randomly ordered pairs, including "equally good"/"both unsuitable"; with few labels additionally a non-neural rank baseline. Later adapter from A or 30–150M edit model trained on before/intent/after. [Diffusion-DPO, Wallace et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12908) is a methodological link for preference fine-tuning, not a ready-made terrain ranker. With small author label sets, start with reranking and uncertainty, not immediately DPO.

**Data/Loss.** DEMs, manually designed interventions, simulation results, and independent model variants mixed; 100–300 initial author pairs as pilot assumption, repeated pairs for consistency. Pairwise ranking loss plus distance to initial landscape, constraint error, and diversity. A differentiable surrogate for floodplain area/runoff can yield search gradients; discrete outlet switching remains non-smooth and must be checked exactly. A second own research variant is differentiable geomorphology: differentiate a bounded diffusion/stream-power solver and optimize material/precipitation fields backward. [Differentiable programming for Earth system modeling, 2023](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/3123/2023/) motivates the approach, but provides no ready terrain editor. Check gradients against finite differences first, initially with fixed flow assignment; on topology changes, compare gradient-free search or re-initialization. Optimizing a soft hydrology penalty is not proof of validity.

**Falsification/PoC.** New landscapes and new target combinations, identical cameras/materials. Human blind judgment and real field metrics must improve; reward increase alone does not count. Search against the same number of random candidates and a direct geometric edit operation; additionally equal GPU time as a second comparison axis. If diversity collapses or search wins only via hidden changes outside the mask, stop.

**Effort/Rights.** Days for candidate search and data collection, weeks for ranker/adapter. Assumption 16–24 GB GPU, 5–20 GPU hours for first candidate batch and ranker pilot; many repeated generations may dominate. Base model license stays active; ranking does not free restricted weights. Document own author labels; no external image-preference data required.

### D · A Regional Latent World with Local Memory

**What does it learn?** Cross-regional morphology and the conditional distribution of fine terrain forms under fixed regional state. Unlike independent tile noise, watersheds, valleys, and neighboring tiles should fit together. Historical interventions live in persistent delta fields and events; a latent alone is not an urban memory.

**Input → Output.** For example, 32–128 km regional elevation/climate with global coordinates, fixed catchments, and local edit journal → local elevation/material fields. Scales are proposals. The published Terrain Diffusion hierarchy is the start for regional consistency; a custom 20–100M detail decoder for 4–8 m would be additional research. Without matching high-resolution training data, generated detail is plausibly invented, not reconstructed measurement.

**Architecture/Training.** Freeze pretrained coarse/base stages; fine-tune local decoder or condition adapter on high-resolution DEM pairs. Add custom 10–50M latent dynamics only if regional change is to be learned on sequences; static latents deliver no evolution. Shared neighbor borders, world coordinates, and overlapping training windows; loss on elevation, gradients, coarse back-projection, and identical overlaps. Geospatial encoder optional as regional context, not a substitute for metric DEMs.

**Falsification/PoC.** Query the same region in a different tile order, after cache clear, and with a shifted window. Then edit a terrace: protected neighbors and existing IDs must stay identical, new local drainage must be consistent. If a local decoder alters the global watershed or global recomputation overwrites accepted neighbors, the persistence claim is refuted. Minimal: 3×3 tiles, one fixed regional field, two edit masks, and stored canonical outputs.

**Effort/Rights.** Days for full pretrained hierarchy, weeks for edit adapter, months for high-resolution latent dynamics. Assumption 24–48 GB GPU, 5–20 GPU hours inference/tile pilot; 30–150 hours as later detail-decoder pilot budget. MIT for Terrain Diffusion code/weights per sources; provenance and rights of additional high-res data separately. Keep old regional versions; model switch creates a new world version.

## Data Are Different Kinds of Evidence

- **Real forms:** [USGS 3DEP](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program/about-3dep-products-services) offers bare-earth DEMs; the [1 m data collection](https://data.usgs.gov/datacatalog/data/USGS%3A4f34caac-f28f-4ea0-8d82-eafb2b8f9a5d) states Public Domain. Record region, acquisition date, horizontal/vertical datum, and accuracy. US landscapes alone do not cover all European valley types.
- **Global forms:** [Major-TOM/Core-DEM, Dataset Card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Major-TOM/Core-DEM/blob/main/README.md) provides 356² crops at 30 m with binary GeoTIFF field `DEM`. Use this field instead of thumbnail/PNG. The source is Copernicus **DSM**, so it also contains vegetation/structures. CC-BY-SA-4.0 per card; additionally document [Copernicus product terms](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/media/files/2025-06/copernicus_contributing_mission_data_access_v2_cop_dem_licenses.pdf) and attribution. An HF metadata license does not replace this check.
- **Synthetic interventions:** control location, strength, and before/after. They verify our own operation, not its frequency in nature. Keep failed generator results as negative examples.
- **Simulations:** provide time, boundary conditions, and balances under named equations. They are process hypotheses and can be systematically wrong. Never call a network trained on them "understands hydrology".
- **Author ranking:** provides aesthetic/gameplay preferences. Do not conflate with natural observation or technical correctness.

Splits by spatially separated catchments and complete sequences, with buffer against overlap leakage. All variants of the same DEM stay in the same split. Also hold out intervention types and material/climate regimes. For pretrained global models, geographic pretraining overlap may be unknown: then claim no true geographic novelty; test targeted new interventions and independent data in addition.

## Compute Costs and Link to the Game

Under 1B is a size bracket, not a cost promise. 300M parameters is about 600 MB FP16 weights. Training with Adam can already take around 16 bytes per trained parameter for weights, gradients, and states, depending on precision scheme; activations, attention, workspace, and data come on top. High resolution, batch, rollout length, and stored gradients can weigh more than parameter count. For diffusion, count NFE per stage and actual denoiser work: disclose CFG, Heun second evaluation, tile overlap, and reruns of rejected candidates. A single-pass decoder does not turn a multi-stage pipeline into NFE1.

Three clear operating modes:

- **Offline/Editor:** Python/GPU generates, simulates, and scores variants. Seconds to hours can be acceptable if the author gains better landscapes. Export canonical fields and accepted changes.
- **Server:** optional asynchronous generation/edit jobs with versioned inputs, model hash, and result file. A server is not a required part of a saved game state.
- **Player:** headless TS/Bun loads canonical data, validates actions, and preserves IDs/events; three.js projects them. Optional browser inference is a separate export/performance pilot and must not block offline research.

Export at minimum with world origin, grid alignment (cells or vertices), spacing in meters, vertical datum, quantized elevations, water/graph references, model/data revision, seed, and hash. Store accepted terrain deltas, boundary fluxes, and affected IDs; replay invokes no inference. A global hydro effect can follow from a local terrain change: measure radius of effect, reject changes outside granted intent or handle explicitly as an additional proposal. Existing-stock identities are not re-invented from image segments.

The [hydrology criteria from 105](../../research/105-kriterien-landschaft-stadt.md#31-landscape-l) apply to adopted game outputs on the **actual export terrain**. Early research may emit formally invalid proposals to learn controllability and failure causes. Report raw output, repaired output, repair volume, and rejection rate separately. Pretty graphics or a passed soft loss target is not a sign-off. The interaction budgets from [100](../../research/100-zielkriterien-kern.md) remain relevant for the game path; they are not an upper bound on an offline training run.
